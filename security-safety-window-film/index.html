Security & Safety Window Film in St. Louis

Thick, clear film that adheres to your glass and holds it together when it breaks, buying precious time against a break-in and keeping shards out of the room. Installed on homes, storefronts, schools and offices across St. Louis City and County.

Security and safety window film is a thick, clear polyester film applied to the inside of your glass. When the glass breaks, the film holds the pieces together in the frame, which slows down a smash-and-grab break-in and keeps sharp shards from flying into the room. It does not make glass unbreakable, but it buys time and reduces injury risk.

What security window film is and how it works

Standard window film is thin, usually around 2 mils (thousandths of an inch). Security and safety films are built from multiple layers of optically clear polyester, laminated together with a strong pressure-sensitive adhesive, for a total thickness of roughly 4 to 15 mils or more. That extra mass and the aggressive adhesive are what make the difference.

When a rock, tool or piece of debris hits filmed glass, the glass still cracks. But instead of falling out in pieces, the fragments stay stuck to the film. Someone trying to get in has to keep hitting, cutting and pulling at a sagging, rubbery sheet of glass, which takes time and makes noise. For a burglar counting on getting in and out in under a minute, that delay matters.

The same principle protects people. If a window breaks during a storm, an accident or vandalism, the film keeps most of the shards attached instead of spraying them across a kitchen, a sales floor or a classroom.

Signs your windows could use security film

  • You have large glass panels on the ground floor, especially facing an alley, parking lot or quiet side street.
  • There is glass right next to a door lock or deadbolt, such as sidelites or a glass storm door.
  • Your business has had a smash-and-grab, a broken display window or repeated vandalism.
  • Basement or garden-level windows sit out of sight from the street.
  • You worry about kids or customers near large panes that could break if someone falls into them.
  • Your building faces open ground where wind-blown debris and hail hit hard in spring storms.

Film thickness and attachment options

Thicker is not automatically better for every window. The right film depends on the glass type (annealed, tempered or laminated), the size of the pane, how the frame is built and what you are trying to protect against. Here is how the common categories compare.

An attachment system anchors the film to the frame, either with a structural sealant bead around the edge or a mechanical bar fastened to the frame. Film alone holds glass together; an anchored system also helps keep the entire filmed pane from being pushed out of the opening in one piece. For storefronts and entrances, it is often the most important upgrade.

Where security film is used

Storefronts and ground-floor commercial glass

Retail windows, glass entry doors and display cases are prime smash-and-grab targets. Film slows entry, and paired with an alarm it gives the alarm time to do its job. We often combine it with our broader commercial window tinting work so one visit covers both security and heat control.

Homes

The most common residential spots are sliding patio doors, glass next to front-door locks, French doors and basement windows. In older homes in St. Louis City and University City, original single-pane glass breaks easily and is worth reinforcing. Many owners in Kirkwood and Webster Groves start with just the back of the house, where windows are hidden from neighbors.

Schools, offices and public buildings

Lobbies, entry vestibules and classroom windows benefit from shard control and delayed entry. Office buildings in Clayton and Creve Coeur often add safety film to lobby glass and ground-floor conference rooms while tinting upper floors for glare.

The St. Louis angle: storms, hail and flying debris

The St. Louis region sees strong spring and summer thunderstorms, hail and tornado-season wind. Hail and wind-blown branches, patio furniture and gravel break plenty of windows every year. Safety film helps contain shards when that happens, so cleanup is easier and the chance of cuts inside the home is lower.

An honest note about storms

Safety film helps hold broken glass together, but it is not a guarantee that a window won't break, and it is not a substitute for storm shutters or taking shelter during a tornado warning. We'll tell you plainly what film can and can't do for your windows.

Combining security with heat, glare or privacy

Security film can be clear, or it can carry a tinted, ceramic or reflective layer. That means a single film can slow break-ins and also cut heat on a west-facing storefront. If summer sun is part of the problem, ask about pairing it with solar heat control window film performance. For basement or street-level windows where you also want to block the view in, a security-grade layer can be combined with privacy window film. Our guide to ceramic vs. dyed vs. reflective window film explains the tint options you can layer in. Performance varies by film, so we show you the spec sheet (TSER, VLT, glare reduction) before you choose.

Our installation process

  1. Estimate and sample on your glass. We come out, check the glass type and frames, measure, and show you film samples on your actual windows.
  2. Film choice with spec sheet. We recommend a thickness and say whether an attachment system makes sense, then give you a written quote.
  3. Glass prep and cleaning. We protect floors and sills, then scrape and clean the glass so nothing gets trapped under the adhesive.
  4. Precise install and edge trim. Thick film is applied and squeegeed firmly, trimmed tight to the frame, and anchored to the frame if you chose an attachment system.
  5. Cure time and cleaning instructions. Thicker films take longer to fully cure; you may see a little haze or small water pockets for a few weeks. We explain how to clean the film and when.

What affects the cost

Pricing depends on the film thickness, the total square footage, the number and size of panes, whether you add an attachment system, how easy the glass is to reach (ladders, lifts, upper floors) and whether you add a tint or solar layer. Lifespan and keeping it clean

Quality security film is built to last many years on interior glass. Clean it with a soft cloth and a mild, ammonia-free cleaner, and avoid razor blades or abrasive pads on the film surface. Ask about our lifetime warranty. Our work is guaranteed to last — if it isn't, let us know and we'll make it right.

Why hire a pro instead of DIY

Thick security film is stiff and unforgiving. It has to go on without trapped dirt, stretched edges or gaps, because any weak spot at the edge is where glass can start to pull out. Attachment systems also require the right sealant and frame prep. A professional install gets the film tight to the frame and fully adhered so it performs the way it is supposed to. Frequently Asked Questions

Does security window film make glass unbreakable?

No. Security film does not stop glass from breaking. It holds the broken pieces together in the frame so an intruder has to keep working to get through, and it keeps sharp fragments from scattering. Its job is to slow entry and reduce injury, not to make a window bulletproof.

How thick should security film be for a storefront?

Many storefronts use film in the 8 to 12 mil range, with thicker film or an attachment system for high-risk glass near doors and display cases. The right choice depends on the glass type, the size of the pane and how the frame is built. We look at your glass in person and show you the options side by side.

Can you see security film on the window?

Clear security film is designed to be close to invisible once it cures. Most people will not notice it from the street or from inside. If you want it to also reduce heat or glare, it can be paired with a tinted or solar layer, which will change the look slightly.

Will safety film help during a hailstorm or tornado?

Safety film can help hold glass together if a window is struck by hail or wind-blown debris, which reduces flying shards inside the room. It is not a guarantee that the glass will not break, and it is not a replacement for storm shutters or sheltering during a tornado warning. Think of it as one added layer of protection.

What is an attachment system for security film?

An attachment system anchors the edge of the film to the window frame, usually with a structural sealant or a mechanical bar. Without it, a whole pane can sometimes push out of the frame in one sheet. Anchoring helps keep the film and glass in the opening longer, which is why it is often recommended for ground-floor commercial glass.

Can I add security film to my sliding glass door at home?

Yes. Sliding patio doors and glass panels next to door locks are some of the most common residential spots for security film. Most patio doors already use tempered glass, so the film mainly helps hold the crumbled pieces in place and slows anyone trying to clear the opening. We confirm the glass type during the estimate.
