Why window film fits University City

University City was founded in 1902, when publisher Edward Gardner Lewis bought land for his publishing headquarters, and it was incorporated in 1906. His 1903 Magazine Building, with its octagonal tower, now serves as City Hall. The city is named for nearby Washington University, and its southern half grew as a streetcar suburb in the 1920s and 1930s, while much of the north side was built after World War II.

That history shows up in the windows. The south side is full of brick homes and apartment buildings from the first decades of the 1900s, many with tall original windows. University City has 16 places on the National Register, including the Delmar Loop–Parkview Gardens Historic District, with Craftsman, Tudor Revival and Georgian Revival buildings, University Heights Subdivision Number One, and part of the private Parkview subdivision it shares with St. Louis City.

Owners of those homes usually want to keep the original sash. Film lets them do that while cutting heat on south and west rooms and protecting woodwork, floors and furniture from UV. On the north side, homes built after World War II bring a different issue: a big picture window or patio slider that turns one room into a hot spot.

Local note: University City historic districts

University City has a Historic Preservation Commission that reviews certain exterior changes in local historic districts and on city landmarks. The city's code lists items like replacing windows or storm sash and modifying window openings. Window film is applied to the inside of the glass and can be removed, but rules vary by district, and a reflective film can change how a facade looks. Check with the city about your address before you install, and we will show you neutral options that keep the windows looking original.

Window film options for University City homes and businesses

Historic and early-1900s homes

Our residential window tinting starts with the rooms that bother you. A light ceramic or spectrally selective solar heat control window film cuts heat without making the glass look tinted, which matters on a brick facade in a historic neighborhood. For drafty single-pane windows, a low-E window film reflects some heat back into the room in winter. If you are deciding between film and new windows, our guide to window film vs. replacement windows lays out the trade-offs.

Apartments and condos

Parkview Gardens and the blocks around the Loop hold a lot of apartment buildings, many close to Washington University. Film here is about privacy and fading. Privacy window film or frosted film on lower panes and bathroom windows lets in daylight while blocking views from the sidewalk. Ask your landlord or association before you install.

Delmar Loop and Olive Boulevard storefronts

The Delmar Loop is a dense strip of restaurants, shops, music venues like The Pageant and the Tivoli Theatre, with the St. Louis Walk of Fame set in the sidewalk. Storefronts here are glass-heavy and face busy foot traffic. Our commercial window tinting pairs neutral solar film with UV fade protection (most quality films block up to 99% of UV) so displays stay visible and hold their color. Clear security and safety window film helps hold broken glass together longer on street-level windows. The same approach works for shops and offices along Olive Boulevard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need approval to add window film to a home in a University City historic district?

Possibly. University City has a Historic Preservation Commission, and its rules list exterior changes such as replacing windows or storm sash that need review in local historic districts. Window film is applied inside the glass and is removable, but rules vary by district, so check with the city's planning and zoning staff about your address before you install, especially for a reflective film.

Will window film work on the old windows in my University City house?

Usually. Many homes in University City's older neighborhoods date to the early 1900s through the 1930s and still have original glass. We inspect each pane during the estimate for cracks, loose glazing and old wavy glass, then recommend a film that suits that glass so you can keep the original windows.

What window film is best for a Delmar Loop storefront?

Most Loop shops want less heat and fading while keeping the display easy to see from the sidewalk. A neutral solar film with strong UV blocking is a common fit, and clear security film can be added to hold broken glass together longer. We check your sun exposure and display layout on-site first.

Can I tint the windows in my apartment or condo near Washington University?

Often, but you will need permission from your landlord or association first, since they usually control exterior appearance. Neutral and frosted films are the easiest to get approved because they change the look the least. We can provide samples and a spec sheet for the owner or board to review.
