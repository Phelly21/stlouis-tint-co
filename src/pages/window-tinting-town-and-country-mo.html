Why window film fits Town and Country

Town and Country was incorporated as a village in 1950 and re-incorporated as a city in 1975, and it was built around a one-acre minimum lot pattern. Its zoning still keeps that character: the Suburban Estate district calls for lots of at least one acre and the Estate district for 80,000 square feet, with green-space requirements that help preserve tree cover. The result is large homes set well back on big, often heavily wooded lots off roads like Clayton Road, Mason Road and Ballas Road.

Those homes tend to have a lot of glass: walls of rear windows facing the woods, two-story family rooms, sunrooms, and big sliding or French doors. That glass creates two specific needs. First, ultraviolet light fades hardwood, rugs, upholstery and artwork year-round, even on a shaded lot. Second, the shading changes with the seasons. Mature trees block much of the high summer sun, but once the leaves drop, the low winter sun can pour straight into rooms that face south or west, with glare to match.

Town and Country is also home to more than houses. Maryville Centre, the office park along I-64, holds multiple multi-story Class A office buildings, and Missouri Baptist Medical Center, Maryville University and several private schools are in the city. The I-64 and I-270 interchange sits on the city's eastern side.

Window film options for Town and Country homes

UV and fade protection

For many homeowners here, fading is the main reason to call. UV and fade protection window film blocks up to 99% of UV and comes in nearly clear versions that don't change the look of the room or the view of the woods. It's a common choice for living rooms, libraries and hallways where art and heirloom furniture sit near the glass. As part of our residential window tinting service, we can match a film to the rooms that need it most rather than treating the whole house the same way.

Low-e film for big glass and cold winters

A wall of glass loses heat in winter. Low-e window film adds a coating that reflects some interior heat back into the room in cold months while still cutting summer solar heat. On a wooded lot where summer heat is less of an issue, low-e film often makes more sense than a dark heat-control tint. If you're weighing film against new windows for an older or drafty home, our comparison of window film vs. replacement windows covers the tradeoffs.

Heat and glare control for open exposures

Not every lot is shaded. Homes that face open lawns or golf course views, or rooms with south and west glass, can still run hot in summer. Solar heat control window film rejects part of the sun's heat and reduces glare. Performance varies by film, so we show you the spec sheet (TSER, VLT and glare reduction) before you choose.

Security and safety film for large glass doors

Big sliding doors and ground-floor window walls on secluded lots are a frequent question. Security and safety window film is a thicker film that helps hold broken glass together, which slows a break-in attempt and reduces the risk of flying shards after an accidental impact. It doesn't make glass unbreakable, and we explain what it can and can't do.

Commercial window tinting in Town and Country

Office buildings along the I-64 corridor have large expanses of glass on multiple floors, which means hot perimeter offices, uneven temperatures and screen glare. Commercial window tinting addresses those problems and can give a building a consistent exterior appearance. We also install film for medical offices, schools and other commercial spaces where privacy, glare and UV are concerns. Privacy window film and frosted film work well for exam rooms, conference rooms and ground-floor offices.
