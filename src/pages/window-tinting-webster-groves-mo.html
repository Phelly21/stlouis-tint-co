Why window film fits Webster Groves homes

Webster Groves was incorporated in 1896 when five railroad communities, Webster, Old Orchard, Webster Park, Tuxedo Park and Selma, joined together. Developers had already been promoting it as the "Queen of the Suburbs," and its tree-lined streets of single-family homes are still what most people picture. Many houses are Victorian or early-20th-century, and a lot of them still have original windows with the slightly wavy glass that owners work hard to keep.

That old glass is a big part of the character, but it is also single-pane, lets in a lot of UV light, and can make a room cold in winter and warm on summer afternoons. Replacing it would change the look of the house. Window film goes on the inside of the existing glass, so it adds UV, heat and comfort benefits while leaving the sashes, muntins and wavy panes in place.

The city's mature canopy helps with summer sun, but it is not total protection. West-facing windows can still catch strong late-day sun under the trees, front porches and bay windows face every direction, and in winter the leaves are gone just as low sun reaches deep into living rooms and onto wood floors and furniture.

Historic districts: check before you tint

Local note on historic preservation

Webster Groves has a Historic Preservation Commission and local historic districts including Central Webster, Webster Park, Marshall Place and Old Webster. Within a local district or on a designated landmark, certain exterior changes require a certificate of appropriateness. Interior window film is generally reversible, but rules vary by property, so if you are considering a tinted or reflective film that changes how your windows look from the street, check with the city first. Nearly clear films are usually the simplest choice for historic homes.

Window film options we recommend in Webster Groves

Clear UV and fade protection

For most older homes, UV and fade protection window film is the first step. It is nearly invisible, blocks up to 99% of UV light, and slows fading of wood floors, rugs, upholstery and the woodwork many Webster Groves homes are known for. From the street, the windows look much the same as before.

Low-E film for original single-pane windows

Low-E window film reflects some heat back into the room in winter and helps reject solar heat in summer, which can make a room with old single-pane windows noticeably more comfortable. It is not a replacement for storm windows or weatherstripping, but it pairs well with them. If you are weighing film against new windows, read window film vs. replacement windows.

Heat and glare control for sunny rooms and newer additions

West-facing rooms, sunrooms, rear additions and patio doors often need more heat control. Solar heat control window film comes in light ceramic and spectrally selective options that cut heat and glare without a dark or mirrored look. Performance varies by film, so we show you the TSER, VLT and glare-reduction numbers before you choose. For older glass, we only recommend films that are compatible with its condition, since some darker, heat-absorbing films can put extra stress on old panes.

Privacy and decorative film

Homes here are often set close together. Privacy window film and frosted or decorative film work well on bathroom windows, side windows facing a neighbor, and front-door sidelights, and some patterns suit older architecture well.
