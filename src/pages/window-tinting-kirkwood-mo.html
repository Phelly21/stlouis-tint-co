Why window film fits Kirkwood homes and businesses

Kirkwood was established in 1853 along the Pacific Railroad and is known as the first planned residential suburb west of the Mississippi River. Its oldest homes, near the historic core around the railroad, include Victorian, Folk Victorian and Colonial Revival styles. Growth after World War II added blocks of brick ranches and modest mid-century houses, and more recent infill has brought new homes with big windows and open floor plans to many streets.

Each of those home types has a different window problem. Original glass in older homes is often single-pane, drafty and hard on furnishings near the window. Mid-century ranches commonly have a big picture window facing the street that heats up the living room. New builds often have large expanses of glass on the back of the house, sometimes facing west, that make kitchens and family rooms uncomfortable on summer afternoons.

Kirkwood also takes preservation seriously. The city has a Landmarks Commission, nine local historic districts such as West Argonne, Savoy and Sugar Creek Ranch, and dozens of designated landmarks, plus districts and properties on the National Register of Historic Places. Window film is a reversible, interior upgrade, which makes it a practical way to improve comfort without touching original sashes. We still recommend checking with the city if your home is in a district and you are considering a visibly tinted film.

Window film options we recommend in Kirkwood

Clear heat and UV control for older homes

For Kirkwood's older houses, most owners want comfort without a tinted look. Nearly clear solar heat control window film and UV and fade protection film cut heat and block up to 99% of UV light while keeping the glass looking much as it did. We check the condition of old panes first, because darker, heat-absorbing films are not compatible with every piece of older glass.

Low-E film for drafty single-pane windows

If you love your original windows but not the cold room behind them in January, low-E window film reflects some heat back into the room in winter and helps reject solar heat in summer. It will not replace storm windows or weatherstripping, but it can be a good addition. Our post on whether window film lowers energy bills covers what to realistically expect, and since Kirkwood is served by its own city-owned utility, Kirkwood Electric, you can compare your own bills before and after.

Heat and glare film for newer builds

Infill homes with large rear windows and sliding doors usually benefit most from stronger heat rejection. Ceramic and spectrally selective films reduce heat and glare while staying light, so you do not lose the view of the yard. Performance varies by film, so we show you the TSER, VLT and glare-reduction numbers before you choose.

Privacy for close-set lots

Many Kirkwood streets have homes fairly close together. Privacy window film and frosted film work well on bathroom windows, side windows that face a neighbor, and front-door sidelights. For more on day and night privacy, see the best window film for daytime and nighttime privacy.

Commercial window tinting in Kirkwood

The Downtown Kirkwood business district along Kirkwood Road and Argonne Drive is listed on the National Register and is full of shops, restaurants and offices, anchored by the 1893 Kirkwood Station on West Argonne and the Kirkwood Farmers' Market just to the east. Storefront glass there deals with afternoon glare, heat and sun-faded window displays. Our commercial window tinting service covers glare and heat control, UV protection for merchandise, privacy film for offices and decorative film for glass doors. For street-facing storefronts in a historic district, we usually suggest lighter films and recommend confirming with the city before going dark or reflective.
