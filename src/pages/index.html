Start with the problem

Which window film fixes what you're dealing with?

Most people don't call about "window film." They call because a room is too hot, the TV is washed out, the hardwood is fading, or the neighbors can see in. Here's what solves each one.

Rooms that bake in summer

Solar & heat control film

Ceramic, spectrally selective and reflective films block solar heat at the glass, so west and south rooms stay closer to the rest of the house.

Cold glass and winter drafts

Low-E window film

Reflects your furnace heat back into the room in winter and still cuts solar heat in summer. A strong fit for older single-pane windows.

Faded floors, rugs and art

UV & fade protection film

Nearly clear film that blocks up to 99% of UV, one of the main causes of fading, without changing how your windows look.

People can see right in

Privacy window film

Reflective films for daytime privacy, frosted and opaque films for privacy around the clock. Bathrooms, street-facing rooms and offices.

Break-ins and storm damage

Security & safety film

Thick, clear film that holds broken glass together, slowing smash-and-grab entry and keeping shards from flying.

Plain glass that needs a look

Decorative & frosted film

Frosted, gradient and patterned film for glass doors, conference rooms, sidelights and shower glass.

Built for St. Louis

Why St. Louis windows need film

St. Louis summers are long, hot and humid, and late-day sun on west-facing glass can push a room well past the thermostat setting. Winters swing the other way, and older single-pane windows in the City and inner-ring suburbs lose heat fast.

Window film works on both problems. Heat control film stops solar heat before it gets into the room, and low-E film helps hold furnace heat inside in January. Curious about the payoff? Read does window film really lower energy bills.

We see the same patterns across the region: brick homes in St. Louis City and Webster Groves with original glass worth keeping, big window walls in Chesterfield and Town and Country, and office glass in Clayton and Creve Coeur that heats up every afternoon.
