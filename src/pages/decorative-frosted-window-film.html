Decorative and frosted window film is a thin film applied to existing glass to create the look of etched, frosted or patterned glass. It adds privacy while letting soft light through, makes clear glass doors easier to see, and can carry custom cut designs for businesses. It is removable and installs in hours instead of the weeks it can take to replace glass.

What decorative window film is

Decorative film is a polyester film with a frosted, textured, patterned or colored finish. It goes on the inside of your glass with a water-based application and bonds as it cures. From a few feet away, a good frosted film reads as real etched glass.

Because it is a film, it can be cut to almost any shape: full panes, borders, horizontal bands, gradients, or shapes and lettering. And if your taste changes, or your lease ends, it can be removed without harming the glass.

Problems decorative film solves

  • Clear glass that leaves a bathroom, entry or office exposed.
  • Glass doors and partitions people do not notice until they walk into them.
  • A plain storefront or reception area that needs branding.
  • Dated glass you want to refresh without replacing.
  • A basement or side window that looks onto something you would rather not see.

Decorative and frosted film styles

Frosted and etched-glass look

The most popular option. Frosted film creates a uniform, soft white finish that blurs shapes in both directions, day and night. It is the go-to for bathrooms, conference rooms and sidelights.

Gradient and fade films

Gradients transition from frosted to clear across the glass. They give privacy at sitting or standing eye level while keeping the top or bottom clear for light and views.

Patterned and textured films

Patterned films add lines, geometric shapes, dots or textures that mimic glass block, reeded glass or seeded glass. They suit front doors, cabinet glass and spaces where you want privacy with more character than plain frosting.

Stripes and bands for glass doors and partitions

Horizontal frosted bands on clear glass doors, storefront entries and office partitions make the glass visible and reduce walk-into accidents. They also give a clean, professional look and add a little privacy at eye level.

Custom cut logos and lettering

Businesses use cut frosted film for logos, suite numbers, hours and simple graphics on doors and windows. Ask us about custom cut designs and we will talk through what works for your glass.

Where decorative film is used

Homes

  • Shower glass and bathroom windows: frosted film for privacy without blinds.
  • Front door sidelights and transoms: frosted or patterned film so the entry stays bright but private.
  • Cabinet glass: soft frosting hides clutter while keeping the glass-front style.
  • Basement windows: obscure an unattractive view, like a window well or neighboring wall.

Pair it with residential window tinting on the rest of the house for heat and glare control.

Businesses

Offices use frosted film on conference rooms, private offices and glass partitions. Retail and restaurants use bands and cut designs on entries. Medical and dental offices frost exam rooms and waiting areas. For whole-building projects, see commercial window tinting.

Removable film vs. replacing with etched glass

Real etched or obscure glass is permanent and can last the life of the window, but replacing panes means ordering glass, removing doors or sashes, and paying for the new glass and labor. In insulated windows, it can mean replacing the whole sealed unit.

Frosted film gives a very similar look on the glass you already have, installs in hours, and can be removed or updated later. For most homes and offices, film is the more practical choice. Weighing bigger window decisions? Read window film vs. replacement windows.

Our installation process

  1. Estimate and samples on your glass. We measure each pane and show frosted, gradient and patterned samples on your actual windows.
  2. Film choice with spec sheet. We confirm the style, layout of bands or designs, and show you the spec sheet for the film.
  3. Glass prep and cleaning. Every pane is cleaned and scraped, which matters most on frosted film where any speck shows.
  4. Precise install and edge trim. Film is aligned, squeegeed and trimmed tight. Bands are measured to sit level across every door.
  5. Cure time and care instructions. We leave you care instructions while the film cures.
