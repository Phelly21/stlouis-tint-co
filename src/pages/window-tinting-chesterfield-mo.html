Why window film fits Chesterfield homes and businesses

Chesterfield is a young city by St. Louis County standards. It incorporated in 1988, and much of its housing is in planned subdivisions built in the decades around that time, from the Baxter Road and Clarkson Road corridors to areas near Kehrs Mill Road and Wild Horse Creek Road. Many of these homes were designed with open floor plans, two-story great rooms, walls of rear windows facing the yard, and arched or half-round transoms that are hard to cover with blinds.

That design brings in a lot of light, and in a Missouri summer it also brings in a lot of heat. A west-facing great room can feel noticeably warmer than the rest of the house by late afternoon, the glare off a TV or kitchen island gets distracting, and hardwood floors and furniture near the glass slowly fade. Window film addresses all three while leaving the view open.

Chesterfield Valley is a different setting. The flat, open land in the Missouri River floodplain was rebuilt after the 1993 flood into one of the region's biggest retail and commercial areas, with Chesterfield Commons, big-box stores, restaurants, offices and the Spirit of St. Louis Airport along Chesterfield Airport Road and the I-64/Highway 40 corridor. Buildings there have few trees around them, so storefront and office glass takes full sun for most of the day.

Window film options for Chesterfield homes

Heat and glare control for great rooms

Our residential window tinting work in Chesterfield usually centers on the rooms with the most glass. Solar heat control window film rejects a share of the sun's heat before it reaches the room, which helps upstairs rooms and great rooms stay closer to the thermostat setting. Performance varies by film, so we show you the spec sheet (TSER, VLT and glare reduction) before you choose.

Keeping the outside look HOA-friendly

Many Chesterfield subdivisions have HOAs with rules on exterior appearance. Rules vary by community, and some may restrict mirror-look film that makes windows read as silver from the street. If that's a concern, we lean toward ceramic and spectrally selective films that stay close to clear glass from outside. Our guide to ceramic vs. dyed vs. reflective window film explains how each type looks and performs. We always suggest checking your covenants or asking your board before installing.

UV and fade protection

Large window walls let in a lot of ultraviolet light, which is a main contributor to fading in hardwood, rugs, upholstery and artwork. UV and fade protection window film blocks up to 99% of UV and can be nearly invisible, which suits homeowners who want to protect a room without darkening it.

Year-round comfort with low-e film

Chesterfield winters get cold, and a two-story wall of glass can make a great room feel drafty even with good windows. Low-e window film adds a coating that reflects some interior heat back into the room in winter while also cutting summer solar heat. It's worth considering for rooms you use year-round.

Newer windows and warranties

Because so many Chesterfield homes have dual-pane or more recent replacement windows, a common question is whether film affects the window warranty. It depends on the manufacturer and the film type. We look at your glass during the estimate, and our post on whether window film will void your window warranty walks through what to check.

Commercial window tinting in Chesterfield

For retail, office and medical buildings, commercial window tinting helps with hot perimeter offices, glare on screens, faded merchandise displays and uneven temperatures between the sunny and shady sides of a building. In Chesterfield Valley, where storefronts face open parking lots with little shade, solar control film is usually the first conversation. Along the office corridors near I-64, Olive Boulevard and Highway 141, glare control and a consistent exterior look across multiple floors tend to matter most.

Some businesses also want daytime privacy for street-level offices or conference rooms. Privacy window film and decorative frosted film can screen views into a space while still passing light.
